Interactive Learning Experience

Spinal Cord Explorer

A clinically integrated, gamified web application for learning spinal cord tracts through interactive visualization, active recall, and lesion localization.

e-Inovasi
Professional Category

Background

Spinal cord tracts are abstract, spatially complex, and clinically high-yield.

Students must integrate pathway anatomy, sensory or motor modality, decussation level, somatotopy, and clinical syndromes. Traditional static diagrams often do not sufficiently support this integration.

Objective

To develop and pilot-test an interactive spinal cord tract learning application that supports:

  • visual pathway understanding
  • active recall and feedback
  • clinical lesion localization
  • mobile-friendly self-directed learning

Innovation

  • Interactive spinal cord atlas
  • Animated tract modules
  • Attempt-first clinical questions
  • Hints after incorrect attempts
  • Know-more explanations
  • Badges, points, certificates

Design Methodology

AnalyseIdentified learning difficulty in neuroanatomy tract localization.
DesignMapped objectives to layered pathway levels and clinical scenarios.
DevelopBuilt web-based dashboard, animations, quizzes, and feedback panels.
ImplementShared with students through browser-based access on personal devices.
EvaluateCollected pilot feedback from 19 student users.

Learning Flow

  • Choose ascending or descending pathway
  • Watch animated tract movement
  • Attempt progressive clinical levels
  • Receive targeted hints and explanation
  • Advance using next-level navigation
  • Track progress and awards

Improvement Priorities

  • More tract labels
  • Flowchart-style pathway summaries
  • More topics and modules
  • Smoother transition between points

Conclusion and Future Scope

The Spinal Cord Explorer was well received by student users and demonstrated strong perceived usefulness for understanding spinal cord tracts. The module combines visualization, active recall, clinical reasoning, and gamification in a scalable browser-based format. Future development will include expanded tract labeling, flowchart summaries, additional neuroanatomy topics, and formal pre-test/post-test evaluation.
